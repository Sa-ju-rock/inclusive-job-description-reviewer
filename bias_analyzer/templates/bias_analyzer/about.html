United Nations Sustainable Development Goals

SDG 5: Gender Equality in the Workforce

Understanding the linguistic barrier between underrepresented talent and leadership or STEM roles.

The Problem Statement

"Unconscious gender bias in job descriptions prevents diverse talent from applying to technical roles."

Decades of occupational psychology research reveal that job descriptions are rarely neutral. Words commonly used in technical postings (such as "dominant", "ninja", "competitive", or "crush") activate agentic masculine stereotypes. Even when women hold identical technical qualifications, they perceive themselves as less likely to belong in organizations using these terms, substantially dampening female application rates in technical and executive pipelines.

Research Foundation: Gaucher et al. (2011)

"Evidence That Gendered Wording in Job Advertisements Exists and Sustains Gender Inequality."
Gaucher, D., Friesen, J., & Kay, A. C. (2011). Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 101(1), 109–128.

The study established three foundational findings that power the EquiText rule-based NLP engine:

  • Occupational Segregation: Job postings in traditionally male-dominated industries (e.g., Software Engineering, Data Science) contain significantly higher concentrations of masculine-coded language.
  • Psychological Belonging: When qualified female applicants read postings with masculine wording, they experience lower anticipated institutional belonging and find the role less appealing.
  • Simple Interventions Work: Neutralizing coded terminology into inclusive, performance-based requirements increases female applications by up to 42% without lowering applicant competence.

Gender Neutrality Index (GNI) Formula

EquiText evaluates job postings through a deterministic mathematical index scaled from 0% to 100%:

# Gender Neutrality Index Calculation

Score = 100 - Imbalance_Penalty - Density_Penalty

Where Imbalance = |Masculine - Feminine| / Total_Coded * 40
And Density = (Total_Coded / Total_Words) * 750 (capped at 60)

90% - 100%: Strongly Gender-Neutral Balanced, objective, and skills-focused phrasing.
70% - 89%: Mild Bias (Low Risk) Slight directional skew with minor word adjustments needed.
50% - 69%: Moderate Bias Noticeable gender-coded jargon that should be revised.
< 50%: High Gender Bias Dense exclusionary buzzwords actively inhibiting diverse talent.
